How to gain confidence in your decisions?

The skill that most of us ignore to learn.

Making decisions is a crucial part of our daily life. Even the AI (Artificial Intelligence) era can’t save us from this, because our decisions are not purely dependent on facts but also on many other emotional factors.

It ranges from deciding what to wear for a specific occasion, which career to choose, or whether to have children or not. When we were young, our parents used to make a lot of these decisions for us. As we grew up, this heavy burden fell on our shoulders.

For some people this transition might happen slowly and without even noticing it. But for some of us it happens so suddenly that we get stuck in this storm. It becomes overwhelming to handle the situation.

If you are such a person then this blog will help you navigate this stormy ocean with some clarity. First question: is it necessary to make these decisions ourselves? Why can’t we go with the flow or let others make those decisions for us?

Yes, that is the easy choice that many of us opt for, but we need to understand the cost behind it. If all of our life’s directions are decided by others, then it defeats the purpose of our life itself.

Also, at some point there will be a situation where you have to decide on something important and you don’t have anyone’s help. It would happen more often than you could imagine.

I was lucky enough that there were people around me during those times. They even made those decisions for me, so I didn’t have to worry about my inadequacy.

I used to be content with whatever I had. A lot of times I used to accept the decision that is considered good by the majority. I considered small decisions insignificant.

It was working fine, until there were situations where I had to make these decisions myself, because no one else could do that for me.

It’s easy to make a decision when there is a clear winner. But, you see, that’s not always the case. There are places where there is no good or bad; rather, it would be just trade-offs.

For example, if you want to buy an outfit for yourself, you would like to choose the one that suits you well. You might like the one which others consider not so good, and vice versa. Here the decision depends on a lot of factors like quality, taste, money, and many other emotional factors.

If you pay close attention to this, then you could understand that the real barrier is the effort. You have to put real effort into making these decisions. If you analyse the situation, then you could find the one that best suits you.

Sometimes you have to spend hours analysing and evaluating possible candidates. But people instead choose the easy way out, that is, to avoid making these decisions. I was also doing the same.

Later I went on a few solo trips over the last few years. Since I was travelling alone, I had to make all the decisions myself — like which cafe to eat at, which hotel to stay in, what places to visit, whom to trust and whom not to, and many more.

I started thinking, what is the best way to make these decisions? How can we gain confidence in our decisions?

During those solo trips, I was forced to make my own decisions, which made me realise that the answer is simple. We need to start by making these small decisions first.

Observe the results, and learn from them. You don’t have to rely on observation alone. You could even use whatever tools are available at your disposal, like books, the internet, AI, or any other help that you could get.

Getting help from people is one of the best things you can do to make this process supercharged. It would help you understand their thought process. But the crucial thing is that the final decision should be yours and yours alone.

Once you’ve learned how to make them, no one else can make these decisions better than you. Each time you make a good decision, it boosts your confidence.

Initially this process might feel overwhelming or confusing to you. You might think, “What if my decision turns out to be wrong?” Trust me, everyone feels the same way at the beginning.

It’s actually okay to make wrong decisions. That’s how you will learn this skill. It will help you next time when you have to make a big one.

So next time when you want to buy an outfit, decide for yourself. If you want to celebrate your friend’s birthday, then decide which cake to buy and where to buy it yourself. This will build your confidence day by day.

You don’t have to do this for years to see the results. Just follow them for a few months and you will notice the change.

Some bonus points: if you are a parent, then teach this skill to your children at the earliest possible age. It is one of the best and most useful things that you could teach them.

Finally, after practicing this for some time, you can make more reliable decisions. When you make a decision, you will have clear reasoning for it. You will start making these decisions with confidence.
