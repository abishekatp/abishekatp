Self-Awareness and Perseverance for Improvement

Two Habits for a Better and More Successful Life

Have we ever thought about why some people are successful in what they are doing, but others are not? Or, just consider people who do things better than we do. If you are curious about how to improve your own life, then this blog tries to help you with that.

Yes, we could find them in all parts of our lives. There are inspiring entrepreneurs, athletes, politicians, scientists, and more.

They might not always be popular ones. They might be our fathers, husbands, wives, sons, daughters, or friends. They don’t always do these things for recognition and appreciation from the world.

Instead, it is their nature. They do it just for their satisfaction. It is not something extra; rather, it’s what they do.

Some people decorate their rooms beautifully, some plan their day more productively, and generally, they do things better than they did previously.

For them, it’s not always a competition to do better than others; rather, it’s an aspiration to find a better way to do things.

Also, we should not have a constant need to do better all the time, because that will make our lives harder, not better. Instead, we should have a consistent habit of checking for better ways to do things without making it a necessity.

It’s not only about doing the same thing better; sometimes it’s adding new routines to our day that can improve our lives.

On the other hand, some people just follow their routines without ever changing them. Every day they wake up and do things the same way they did yesterday.

There is nothing wrong with that unless we are expecting some change in our lives. Doing the same job and eating the same breakfast every day until some external force changes things won’t improve our lives in any way.

There might be better tools to make our job easier and more efficient. Our breakfast could be portioned differently to meet our nutritional goals or to become healthier. But we will never know about them until we search for them.

I asked myself what makes these people different from others. How do they do things better? I started observing people around me. That’s when I noticed a pattern: these people are persistently making a conscious effort to make things better.

When they do this consistently over the long term, it becomes their second nature.

There are two steps involved in this process. First, we should be very self-aware about what we are currently doing and think about what the better ways are to do that.

Second, and even more important, is perseverance to follow through even when people around us might consider it impossible. We can take feedback from people, but we shouldn’t consider it the ultimate truth.

When people do this successfully on a large scale, they become very successful in whatever they are trying to achieve.

Take, for example, Elon Musk; he wanted to make electric cars better when no one thought it was feasible. He wanted to build reusable rockets when people thought that it was impossible.

He didn’t do all these things just for money or for success; if we observe his interviews, we can understand that he has a natural interest in, and curiosity about, making things better.

A lot of engineers and scientists in his company must also have the same mindset to achieve those results.

So I am not saying this skill is only useful for grand success; that’s not the purpose. After all, there are many people trying to achieve what Elon is trying, and not everyone gets the same result.

There is some level of randomness and luck involved in it. But there is almost always some advantage for people who try to do things better over those sitting idle.

Suppose we are saving money in our personal account. Some of us may settle for that option permanently; instead, what if we don’t close that door and are curious about better options?

We can learn and adapt to new investment strategies, like buying a piece of land or gold, investing in a mutual fund, investing in a new business, or a mixture of these things.

It’s not just about our lives alone; we could even try to improve the lives of others. Suppose we see that people who live in the hills don’t get access to advanced medical support.

Ask ourselves: how can we make their lives better? This is how some of the great startups emerge in our world.

We could build a more affordable drone that can carry patients more quickly to hospitals. People are already trying these approaches, so it’s just an example.

The core idea is this: we shouldn’t just settle for our current lives; we should be curious about how we can improve our lives and the lives of others.

Self-awareness is very critical to this; we can’t change anything unless we know what to change. So maybe every week we can spend an hour analysing what we are doing currently and writing down better ways to do it.

We can get feedback from people, analyse the options, and try to implement a feasible solution. If it’s not working, we shouldn’t give up on the idea itself.

Maybe we can refocus on something else for some time and come back to this problem later when better tools are available.

These two habits will take us far closer to a better and more successful life than many other things.
