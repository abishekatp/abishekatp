Listening to the Language of the World

The Alchemist tells us to listen to the language of the world. Is there actually something like that?

Recently I read the book The Alchemist. The author tells us to listen to the language of the world. He speaks about this one thing in multiple places repetitively. When we speak the language of the world, it will guide us through life.

I was thinking about it for some time, “is he saying something totally new?” No, not at all. For a long time, many people have been telling us to listen to the world, listen to the things around you, it will teach you a lot more than any other education could.

Is there actually something like a language of the world? If yes, what is it trying to tell us? Is it telling us to live a carefree life or is it telling us to fight for our dreams? Is it trying to show us how beautiful or kind it is, or is it telling us how cruel it could be?

You see, even though I try to listen to things, I don’t always succeed. I used to be the guy who thinks about the next thing to say without giving much effort to listening. If that happens to you also, then you are not alone. We focus so much on how to express ourselves that we are forgetting about the most important thing, which is listening. We have a lot of people teaching us how to speak or present ourselves, but not many are telling us how to listen.

I think it’s because listening is more unique and complex; listening is not as simple as speaking. Even though we call it the language of the world, each one speaks to the world in their own language. This language is constrained by one’s knowledge and experiences. The same thing may tell different stories for different listeners.

I am not talking about just sitting and looking at things as they happen. It is more about intentionally listening to the things that are happening around us. In my case, a lot of the time, in the middle of something that I am doing, I start daydreaming. People used to tell me about this, but what can I do? I am trying my best, but all I need is a small distraction to take me to my own dream world. So for me, listening needed to be more intentional.

So sometimes I intentionally put away all of my distractions and try to listen to the world. Indeed, the world speaks in its own language and it has never disappointed me. Whenever I try to listen, it calms my mind, it teaches me something new, it shows me how beautiful it is, or sometimes it tells me how cruel it could be. You see, this is where you see your own truth. It speaks with you honestly. It doesn’t lie to you unless you want it to.

It is not a twenty-second reel which is specifically crafted for your attention, or exactly a hundred-and-fifty-word paragraph which is written to persuade people. Also, it is not a three-hour movie which tries to make us sit in that chair the whole time. These things only show us what others want us to see.

There are only a few people who really want to show us the truth, and even for them, it’s their own understanding of the language of the world. So for you to understand the world, you need to intentionally listen, not just skim through things.

For example, when I see a boy walking on the street holding his father’s hand, it tells me how precious those moments are. When a woman is sewing dresses on the pavement under a big umbrella, it tells me about her hardship for her family. When a man runs towards his family with a handbag in one hand and a food parcel in the other, it tells me about his love for his family.

When I see a small girl and boy doing gymnastics in the train for some money, it tells me how cruel life could be. When I see a husband and wife who are blind, selling pens and other things, it tells me about both cruelty and love at the same time. These same things might tell totally different stories for different people.

That’s one of the reasons people tell us to travel — to listen to the reality of the world. The world doesn’t know beauty, kindness, or cruelty. It is us who name those things. The language of the world only knows how to be honest. It is us who miss listening to its language; it’s us who try to hide its truths.

So all I want to say is, try to listen to the world — it tells you the truth, and it will be your true companion for this journey of life.
