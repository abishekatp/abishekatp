To Kill A Mockingbird - Reflections

The Story That Is Hollering The Foundation of Our Society

Recently I read the book “To Kill A Mockingbird” by Harper Lee. My brother had recommended it. Initially I was not much interested in it, but that title seems interesting, doesn’t it? I was wondering who that mockingbird is? Why did the author choose this title? Before coming to that let me outline the book first.

Outline of the book

The first thing I wanted to talk about was the characters in this book. The author had developed the characters so well that I was connected with them very well. In my childhood, I spent some time with my sisters. So the character Jean Louise Finch was very close to me.

She was an eight year old girl who lived with her father, elder brother and Calpurnia (a black woman). Calpurnia was a black woman who worked in their house as a maid. Jean Louise was also called by the name Scout by her brother and father. That was very cute in my opinion.

She was the one who narrated this story to us. It was a wise and well thought decision by the author. She could have chosen any other character, but she specifically chose a girl who was going to enter a world full of restrictions soon.

Another thing that I loved a lot was the relationship between Scout and her brother. Usually it’s fun watching kids for me. It didn’t feel like I was reading a book. I could still see them playing in their front yard. Also the character Dill added more friend vibe to that trio. The three of them were so cute and fun together.

Among all I admired the relationship between Atticus Finch and his children the most. Specifically how he handled their questions.

Especially the curious questions from his daughter Scout. Her curiosity and understanding of the world was a very interesting aspect of this story that took me to the world of Maycomb town.

The scenes where Atticus tried explaining to her the social hierarchies in their society, the definition of rape, said more about Atticus than any direct description could do.

Also the way he handled Dill when he ran away from his parents’ house and in many other places the author was clearly showing us how good parenting should look like.

Whenever his daughter or son had asked him some uncomfortable questions, he tried to answer them with utmost sincerity. He didn’t try to hide the truth with the assumption that they could not understand it.

The time when a gang of people tried to harm Atticus, and Tom Robinson’s courtroom scenes, were really emotional. When their children asked about these events, Atticus explained to them about how unpredictable people could be and how unfair the world could be.

He was a kind and calm man who didn’t react to people’s actions, but rather analyzed their reasons. He actually put himself in their shoes before reacting.

Now if you are still reading I want to give a strong spoiler alert. I am going to reveal a lot of spoilers from now on, so if you want to read the book yourself then don’t read this blog any further.

The Title of the Story

I didn’t want to explain the whole story here, because it would take more than a single blog to do that. Even if I tried, it wouldn’t give the same experience as the one the author did in the book.

I wanted to focus on the title and how it connected so well to the story. It was not directly implying the moral of the story. You could say that was a poetic way to tell the conclusion.

I couldn’t think of a more suitable name for this novel. Initially when I started reading the book I was wondering who that mockingbird could be. I had some speculations, but to my surprise there was not a single but two mockingbirds in this story.

One of them was Tom Robinson, a black man with one useless arm, who was falsely convicted for the rape crime and shot dead when he tried to escape from the prison. A man named Bob Ewell who lived near the black neighbourhood accused Tom of raping and assaulting his daughter.

Even though his daughter tried taking advantage of Tom, she had told a different story in court, either intentionally or unintentionally, because of the pressure from her father or guilt of societal judgment. But as a result an innocent life was lost at the end.

If there was a white man in that place, he would have gotten justice. The author very clearly articulated this situation from the beginning. The social pressure surrounding the prejudice against black people was very clear to the reader.

The second mockingbird was Boo Radley. It was not a big surprise but the way it ended was an interesting one. His real name was Mr. Arthur Radley.

From the start I had a speculation that he might be the mockingbird. But Tom seemed to be a more suitable candidate. But in the end both of them were mockingbirds.

From the start the author described him as the mysterious man who lived next door to Atticus. He never came out of the house. There were a lot of times Scout, Jem and Dill tried to uncover that mysterious man and failed each time.

Mr. Arthur interacted with children here and there in the entire story. It showed that he cared about Scout and Jem for some reason. But he never revealed himself explicitly. The whole neighbourhood assumed him to be of an evil character. Each one had their own evil story about him.

In the last chapter, Bob Ewell came after the children of Atticus for revenge. The mysterious Boo Radley finally revealed himself to save them.

This scene took place in the dark and Scout didn’t have clear visibility of what was happening there. They were coming back from school after the Halloween party.

After this chaos Arthur Radley carried Jem home and Scout followed him. She only figured out that he was Boo Radley after his interactions with the Doctor and her father.

When Ewell tried to harm Scout, he died mysteriously, stabbed by a kitchen knife. Sheriff Tate analysed the crime scene and wanted to conclude it as an accident during the defense by Jem.
Initially Atticus tried to persuade him to take this to court, but after some convincing by Tate he accepted to close this as a self-defense and accident.

So we don’t know clearly whether Boo killed Ewell or he fell on his own knife, but if this case goes to court, Boo has to come out to the public. He would have faced similar consequences as Tom Robinson.

Here my understanding was that if Mr. Arthur came out to the court, the legal system would kill another mockingbird just because of its prejudice. Even if Boo Radley had killed Ewell in defense and saved the lives of children, society wouldn’t accept him as a hero.

As the story came to an end, there were not just one but two mockingbirds. One of them was killed and there was another one who was saved.

The Moral of the Story

We could ask why Tom Robinson had to escape from prison. After all, Atticus gave him confidence that there is a good probability that he will win the appeal. But at some point Tom had told Atticus that he knew from the moment he got arrested that he was a dead man.

The justice for the black man was not the same as for the white man. He had lost trust in the judicial system. This was the same reason Atticus and Sheriff Tate didn’t take the Bob Ewell case to court.

Even though this novel was written in 1960, it is still relevant. We say that everyone is equal in front of the law, but it is not always true. The prejudice and corruption in our society still make it hard to get justice.

In today’s world the court and law are not the same for rich and poor; it is not the same for the man in power and a common man. That’s the reason these days we see a lot of people take the law into their own hands and try to punish whoever they think is bad.

We must understand one thing here: the justice system is the foundation of our society. If people start to distrust the justice system, then the whole society will collapse sooner or later. It won’t happen overnight; it won’t be visible to our eyes, but it will slowly erode our society like a termite in a beautiful wood house.

As a society our duty is not only to create our laws with sincere moral standards, but also to abide by that law and ensure that everyone gets justice regardless of their race, caste, religion, money, power, status or anything like these.
