Don’t Be A Validation Seeker

Without This Realisation, It Would Make Your Life Miserable

Seeking validation from others- have you ever thought about this? It destroys our lives in ways that we can’t even imagine if we don’t pay enough attention to it.

Wanting validation is normal human nature, but it becomes an issue when it starts to excessively affect your relationships, decisions, and daily life in a significant, persistent way. The following checklist will help you understand your status on this.

  • Being a people pleaser. Trying to be good to everyone all the time. It’s not the same as being kind to other people.
  • Constantly overthinking about each and every text reply or social media reaction from your friend or partner. When your friend doesn’t like your post, it becomes a war room inside your mind, analysing all the possible reasons.
  • Comparing yourself to others to know your self-worth. Your self-worth should not depend on how other people define their success.
  • Staying in an unhealthy relationship just because it approves of who you are. You define your self-worth based on whether someone loves you or gives you attention.
  • Trying to achieve some goal just for the recognition from it. You are not enjoying the achievement itself, instead a recognition that comes out of it. When you don’t get such recognition or reaction, you start to feel unhappy, anxious, or feeling empty in life.

This list will not be exhaustive. But the most efficient way to figure this out would be to ask the following question.

“Does your mood, happiness, decisions, actions, and daily life largely depend on other people’s reaction or approval?”

If yes, then you need to reconsider how you value yourself. But why do we do this?

Humans are social animals, and they live in groups. When people from the same group don’t agree with them or approve of their behaviour, it greatly influences their survival in that group. So seeking validation makes them feel safe in it.

Even today, it is true; the only difference is that we live in big societies. Also, we have constant influence and comparisons from social media. This has only amplified this effect. We attach our self-worth to these external validations.

Let me tell you my personal story about how this has affected me in the past.

I used to be a person who constantly sought validation from others. When I was studying in high school, one time my cousin told me that I was not at the top of the class. I started worrying and crying about that. At that time, my self-worth was attached to being at the top of my class.

Unfortunately, I became the topper of my class that year, and for all subsequent years until college. If that didn’t happen, I might have re-evaluated my self-worth at that time.

In some way, the pressure from external validation kept me pushing to focus more on my studies. But it was not a healthy way to achieve that goal. It made me constantly worry about things. Also, it was secretly waiting to destroy me.

Instead of trying to find a passion for myself, I focused on things that everyone approved of. So even though on the outside it seemed like a success, personally it was not satisfying.

To become a doctor was my only goal during my school time. At the end of my school days, I couldn’t become one. It destroyed me. I constantly worried about that, blaming myself for it. It affected my focus on my college education.

I was trying to cope with that stress by setting different goals and chasing different dreams. But I never realised there was something going on underneath all this chaos. So it followed me wherever I went like a shadow.

But the real breakthrough came when I realised the following: I have always used these external validations to define my self-worth. I only followed things that everyone approved of.

If I had tried to fight this enemy without spotting him, I would not have known where to aim my weapon. So now I started fighting this head-on. Whenever I notice these kinds of triggers, I try to calm myself and rethink my actions.

To understand how I tackled this, let’s take another scenario that I faced after identifying this issue. When I started writing on Medium, I asked myself, ” Is this another way of me seeking external validation?

To understand that, I needed to know the reason behind the activity: one, to make myself a better person by improving my thought process and communication; second, trying to do something meaningful in this life that would help other people, even when I am gone.

I had to make sure that I would do this even when no one recognised me, or when people disagreed with me. Many such events happened during this writing process, but I still wanted to do this. That’s when I knew that I was doing this for myself.

You see, an old habit can’t be cut off just like that. Sometimes when I publish something, I check whether someone commented positively or gave claps for it. But I wanted to be careful about these observations.

Consciously, I started making sure those reactions do not decide whether I write or not, the topics I write about, and the opinions I express.

Similarly, while posting something on social media, initially I used to strongly value how people reacted to it. But now I tell myself that I am just using this tool to store my memories or best moments and nothing more than that. So whether someone likes it or not does not change that value.

It doesn’t mean you should not value other people’s opinions, but rather it should not influence every decision you make in your life, and it should not define your self-worth.

Before ending this blog, I wanted to share a set of rules that would be helpful to tackle this problem.

  • First, identify the situation. This needs conscious evaluation of your thoughts and actions.
  • When you feel sad, anxious, confused, or uneasy about something, is it because of other people’s reaction or your own concern?
  • If it’s all about other people’s reactions, approvals, or likings, then first acknowledge that feeling.
  • Reassure yourself that you do not always need external validation to be happy, make decisions, or live your life. Your life’s value doesn’t always depend on others.
  • Take action based on how you actually feel about it, not because of external pressure.

What I am trying to say is that try to build your own identity, hobbies, and life that would not largely depend on others’ reactions or approvals. Some practical steps could be as follows.

  • Make small decisions without relying on others, like what to eat, what to wear, or which movie to watch.
  • When there is no reply from your friend, don’t overthink about it. Instead, focus on your own priorities.
  • Next time your boyfriend or girlfriend leaves you, or when you are laid off from your job, don’t consider that the end of your life. Acknowledge that a small setback, learn from it, and move forward with your life.

This way, you can slowly detach yourself from the validation-seeking behaviour.
