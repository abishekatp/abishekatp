Reflections on "Why Were Women Enslaved?" by Thanthai Periyar

Radical, Rational and Revolutionary

This book was published based on a collection of writings by Thanthai Periyar as part of the Self-Respect Movement from 1925 to 1973. Some of these ideas might seem simple today, but in 20th-century India, they were greatly radical, rational, and revolutionary.

This book mainly focuses on women’s freedom, but it is not intended for women alone. Men and the idea of Masculinity are the main reasons for the suppression of women’s freedom.

So all men should try to read, reflect, and correct their ideas about women’s freedom. In that sense, this book encourages us to rethink how we approach individual freedom.

I think we should use the scientific way of looking into all the aspects of our lives.

Whatever is right for men should be right for women, and vice versa is also true. That is true equality.

When considering equal rights, biological differences or any social reasons should be insignificant. These differences should rather be used to protect their rights instead of suppressing them.

We might assume that a lot of these ideas have already been implemented, but in reality, they are still followed in many parts of the country and the world in modern forms and names.

We could see that people are using social media as a platform to influence people in the wrong direction using their mythological and imaginative ideas and reasoning.

Some of them are followed as tradition or social norms; some of them are argued using pseudoscience and false assumptions about gender biology.

People might say that naturally men and women are not equal. But first consider whether we are living by nature in all aspects of our lives.

Even though there might be some biological differences, nature doesn’t signify in any way that women are inferior to men.

On the other hand, a lot of people don’t even think about these ideas. Even when they think about them, they are not ready to talk about them; even when they talk about them, they are not ready to practise them.

The purpose of this blog is not to preach these ideas alone, but rather to ask people to be more radical, rational, and logical in their assumptions and perspectives about the world and our lives.

Don’t blindly follow everything that society tells you; instead, use your radical and rational thinking to analyse what is right or wrong.

The book is available in both Tamil (Pen En Adimaiyanal?) and English (Why Were Women Enslaved?). If you get really curious, give it a try; it’s really a very short book.

Chastity

Whether chastity is a moral necessity is a different topic; the real problem is that in many societies chastity is only imposed upon women.

If we assume that chastity is important for being morally good, then shouldn’t it be imposed on both genders? There is no natural reason that says it is only for women. This is the question that the author tries to argue in this chapter.

In reality, the term chastity has been used to control and suppress women. People find different reasons, like tradition, God, religion, and even science, to support this idea.

A man can have multiple relationships with women; people don’t blame those men, and sometimes they are praised for their ability to maintain multiple relationships.

On the other hand, the same action done by women would be considered immoral, impure, and hateful. Especially, women themselves support and advocate for this double standard.

Reflection:

My take on this chapter is that, instead of considering chastity as an absolute truth that can only be imposed on women, we would rather leave that as an individual preference of both genders.

Valluvar and Chastity

This chapter argues against people who are using Thiruvalluvar (an Indian philosopher and Tamil-language poet) and similar intellectual figures for their argument.

He argues that even great intellectual people can make mistakes. They would have been largely influenced by their culture and society at their time.

Just because they are intellectual on specific topics doesn’t mean they are correct in everything.

Don’t assume everything someone says is correct; we should analyse their ideas, take what is relevant for us today, and leave the things that don’t make sense anymore.

This problem only arises when we worship people like a god and their statements as absolute truth.

He advises that we shouldn’t accept everything in the name of God or religion itself. We should question them also.

Reflection:

Even today, the problem is arising in new forms like influencers, hero worship, social media, religion, caste, and sugar-coated rules for women.

What I think is that we shouldn’t trust anyone blindly; instead, we should use our rational thinking to accept only what is right and works for us.

This includes even Periyar himself, because the upcoming chapters about love and birth control might seem very controversial.

Instead of accepting them blindly, we should analyse his reasons and accept things that meet our own set of values.

Similarly, instead of imposing our own set of values on everyone, we should respect everyone’s individual preferences and choices.

Love

He explains that people assume that love is something magical, and we romanticize it so much that we lose track of what it actually is.

He argues that love could not be defined without affection, physical or psychological desire and need, or some kind of mutual benefit. So love is nothing more than any of these feelings.

He argues against suppressing people’s individual freedom in the name of rules constructed around love. He states that this would only happen when we consider love as pious and unchangeable.

Reflection:

This chapter is not against love, but rather the suppression that happens in the name of love.

“We can’t love anyone else after loving one person” doesn’t make any sense. I personally agree with this fact. But we should leave that as a personal choice rather than a general rule for everyone.

Also, for some people, love can be magical and spiritual; there is nothing wrong with that as long as it doesn’t affect other people’s individual values, rights, or freedom.

When we are searching for our partner, we should be clear about our expectations and beliefs about love and marriage.

Don’t let society or others set these rules for you. You can always take opinions or feedback from others, but you should be the one making the final decision.

Divorce

He explains that marriage is a contract between two people for their mutual benefit, comfort, and understanding. Marriage builds upon the idea of love for a more concrete agreement between two people.

The problem arises when we consider marriage or love as something holy or pious. In that case, we force people to be together even when all the odds are against it.

So he argues that marriage is used as a device for women’s enslavement.

He argues that divorce should not be considered something immoral or a sin. When one of them is always negatively affected by that relationship, there is no natural reason to continue that marriage.

People may argue that if a man leaves a woman after marriage, then what would be her situation?

When women are truly free from their restrictions, there is no reason for anyone to take care of them. If they want someone in their lives, there shouldn’t be any restriction on their remarriage either.

Reflection:

I think the idea of marriage itself is invented by us for our own comfort and goals. So there should be nothing wrong with ending that marriage when those are affected.

But we should also be aware that there will always be some incompatibility between two people. So divorce for every small difference between a couple would be the wrong way to understand this ideology.

I think the author is trying to talk about real concerns like continuous psychological or physical torture by one partner, or two people having totally different sets of values and goals in their lives.

For example, no one should be forced to live with a partner who is a drug addict and not ready to change.

Or a person may expect more than what a partner can provide in terms of financial, physical, or psychological needs. Like this, there can be a lot of complicated reasons for divorce.

So there is nothing wrong with divorce as long as it doesn’t discriminate against one side.

Remarriage is Not Wrong

Building on top of the previous chapter, he mainly focuses on remarriage while the first partner is still alive.

Initially, he specifically supports men getting remarried, but at the end he states that whatever he argued for men will be the same for women also. Both of them have equal rights in this.

Society generally approves of remarriage when the wife is dead or critically ill. But it curbs remarriage when there is an incompatibility of any kind between two people.

Reflection:

What I think is that if the sole reason for marriage is the mutual benefit of both people, then when it is not working out, there is no reason to force this relationship.

Instead, they can separate and find better partners to lead their lives.

Prostitution

Whether prostitution is right or wrong is a separate moral discussion, but the actual concern is that the term itself is only used to disrespect women.

The vocabulary around prostitution is generally used to disrespect women, not men. When a man runs a brothel, he can roam freely with dignity, but not the women who work there.

A woman is called a prostitute when she has physical relationships with men other than her husband.

But the set of rules to classify a woman as a prostitute itself varies based on different societies and time periods.

Some societies allow a woman to get married and have relationships with more than one man. But when she has a relationship with someone outside that group, she will be considered a prostitute or an equivalent term.

Reflection:

Even today, women who have relationships with men of a specific caste or race are disrespected with these terms.

But the same rules don’t apply to men. In a lot of cases, a man who has a relationship with multiple women will consider that a matter of pride, in the same society where similar women would be considered sinful or immoral.

I think all these double standards only signify that women are some kind of slaves or objects to be handled by men. They should be eradicated for true equality and freedom of women.

The State of Widows

At the time of writing, there was very strong resistance against widows getting remarried, regardless of their very young age.

India was just starting to get out of the self-immolation of women after their husbands’ deaths and child marriages.

Currently, there is a lot of improvement in this area, but there is still some kind of psychological, religious, or social pressure against women who want to get married after the death of their husband.

In most cases, these rules don’t apply to men when their wives are dead, even in olden times.

So society should understand and accept that there is nothing wrong with getting married again after the death of the husband.

Women have their own physical and psychological needs just like men; trying to suppress them for any reason does no good to anyone.

Reflection:

When we understand the author’s argument against love and marriage, this point will become very obvious.

So this also should be left as an individual preference rather than a general rule for everyone.

The Property Rights for Women

In the early 20th century, there was no legal requirement to give property rights to women in India and many parts of the world.

He argued that when women are given equal rights to property as men, that would be a significant step towards women’s freedom.

When women have their own property and wealth, it would decrease the dependency on others.

Also, there is nothing inherent or natural about denying their equal rights to property. Considering them not worthy of property rights is a kind of discrimination toward them.

Reflection:

Even though constitutional rights and laws mandate this today, in the name of tradition, religion, and culture, women are still denied property inheritance from their parents.

Also, people consider dowry as family pride these days. I would ask people to give their daughters equal rights to whatever they have; that will empower them more than a dowry they pay during the marriage.

Birth Control

In this chapter, he argues against childbirth. He doesn’t argue this in terms of beauty or health, but rather for women’s freedom.

When a woman is married to a man and has kids, her freedom is restricted at many levels because of those children. Even when she wants to leave that person for some reason, most of the time children become the reason to stay.

Also, he argues this rule generally applies to both men’s and women’s freedom. Even men face a lot of restrictions on their agency due to children.

Reflection:

I personally think there is also a beautiful side to children and family. So instead of blindly following a single rule, try to analyse your situation.

He doesn’t argue against having children entirely. But he was trying to explain that having children largely supported women’s enslavement at that time, so he advised women not to do that until they get that freedom.

If having a child will affect your own freedom and agency, then don’t have one until you are confident enough that it won’t happen.

But you should know that there will always be some level of responsibility that comes with children for both parents.

You should decide the things that you are ready to sacrifice to have children.

For Women’s Freedom, “Masculinity” Should Die

Finally, he advises not to trust the men who just speak for the freedom of women. The majority of the time, those are just feigned dramas to impress a woman or to achieve whatever they want from you.

A lot of men speak of feminism in society and don’t follow it in their personal and family life.

Society attributes courage, anger, strength, and authority to men. It attributes affection, care, patience, and tolerance to women. This is wrong in many ways. Here, the definition of “Masculinity” itself is constructed upon the discrimination of women.

But what we miss seeing is that, just like restrictions on education and authority for specific castes or races of people, women have historically been denied education and authority.

To understand this, first realise that biological differences between women and men don’t signify that one is superior to the other.

Just because men have slightly more strength and women are capable of bearing children doesn’t mean one should allow the other to be suppressed.

The terms “Masculinity” and “Femininity” are social constructs that can change over time, geography, culture, and society.

In most cases, these terms are used to signify “Femininity” as something inferior. For the same reasons, the author argues that for women to be free, the idea of “Masculinity” should die.

Reflection:

I personally think there are differences between men and women, but those differences don’t signify that either is superior. Instead, we should rethink what masculinity and femininity are.

Also, men should really come forward to accept their mistakes and honestly try to support the freedom of women. We should not use false promises to convince women for our own advantage.

Our final take is that even though there can be biological differences between men and women, they should be used only to ensure equality, not the other way around.

One thing is that biology alone doesn’t define who we are. Our current world has been constructed by overcoming a lot of biological obstacles.

We didn’t give up during COVID, the flu, and similar situations. Then why should we accept biology as the reason for discrimination against women alone? Similar to how we provide maternity leave for women during childbirth, we can devise ways to ensure equality.

So we need to work together to respect the differences, ensure equality, and protect the freedom of every individual.
