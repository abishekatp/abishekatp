Why Do I Still Write In This AI Era?

You Don’t Always Write For Others

It’s been some time since I got a dopamine hit after solving a hard problem at work. There are no Aha! moments anymore. That used to happen at least once or twice each week. A week would have been incomplete without one.

If you are someone who works with software like me, then have you ever spent hours trying to figure out why the heck the website is taking too long to load? At the end, the culprit would have been the data format. We would have used JSON where CSV was the right fit.

But nowadays, those eureka moments are put to the grave. The applications that took weeks to build, now only take hours. Some bugs that took hours to fix, now only take a couple of chats.

Here I want to be cautious. This might not be the case for everyone. The tools I work with are mostly internal. But day by day, this has been happening very rapidly to everyone.

Either way, no one can argue with the fact that AI has increased productivity tremendously. But at what cost? Those eureka moments were the rewards that kept us going.

Each and every day, the creative and problem-solving part of my work is being sucked out. So I was searching for something meaningful in life.

Writing has become my go-to place whenever I want to clear my mind. Before finding the Medium or the AI era, I used to write on GitHub about some technical stuff. Even before that, I used to keep a lot of personal journals and notes about things I learnt.

If you are someone who spends hours or days creating or crafting what you love, this world has new challenges for you. It could be writing, drawing, music, video editing, coding and anything else. The AI is crushing on all of those fronts.

Today, people are generating tons of blogs, videos and images using AI and publishing them. Then what’s the point of spending hours writing these blogs?

Also, AI-written content is becoming more and more useful when it is crafted wisely. So I was asking myself, why do I have to write anymore?

AI helps me in my daily work and even in my writing. English is my second language. So I rely on AI to learn new words, do quick research and edit my blogs. But does everyone have that line? What if someone is just generating AI content and posting it online?

Earning through writing has become even tougher now with these new dynamics.

Though a small optimistic child in the corner of my heart is screaming to become a great and famous writer, common sense is telling me not to have big hopes for that career path.

So I have to work a nine-to-five job to pay my bills and spend my extra hours on crafting, editing and publishing these blogs or any of my writing projects.

There have always been two kinds of art people. The one who does it for money and the one who does it for the sake of just doing it.

The gap between them used to be very small. But the AI era just placed a Pacific Ocean between them. Whether we like it or not, it is happening.

So the solution is to think of writing as a way to express my thoughts, emotions and perspectives about the world. Writing became an exercise to train my mind. A way to organise my thoughts and clearly express or communicate them.

Writing is helping me to build a better version of myself each and every day. So I think the purpose of any art should be the same, that is, to realise a better version of ourselves.
