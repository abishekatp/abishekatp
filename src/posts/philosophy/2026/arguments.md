---
title: Arguments Are Not Actually Bad, But The Way You Approach It Could Be
date: 2026-08-30
description: How did I learn to use arguments as a way to grow?
---

Have you ever argued with someone just to prove that you're right? But deep inside you knew that you were wrong. If you are someone who will stand your ground blindly, then this blog is for you.

On the outside it might seem like confidence. After all, you should be the one advocating for your ideas and values. Who else will do it for you?

But that doesn't mean you need to be arrogant about it. You should know where to be confident and where to be humble and open-minded. That wouldn't happen without deliberately practicing it.

Personally, I used to argue with my brother and friends a lot. It would be on arbitrary topics like technology, politics, or some personal things. During one such argument, my brother asked me this: "Do you realize what you are doing? You always try to prove yourself right and do not listen to the other person."

On that day, I replied to him that I understood it and was trying to improve myself. But deep inside I believed that I was right most of the time. The irony is that even if it's true, there is no use if I can't communicate it properly.

Later, some of my friends also told me the same. So I started thinking about it and wanted to really improve myself. Initially, I tried to avoid some of these arguments. But that was not a solution. Just because there are accidents on the road, we can't avoid our daily commute.

Naturally, I wanted to express my opinions on things. You see, a lot of times arguments are a necessary part of our life. That is a way and an opportunity to persuade others in a particular direction and grow. So first we will try to understand the reasons for any argument.

The first reason might be that you are wrong and still defending yourself out of ego or fear of accepting the truth. The other reasons might be that the other person is wrong or both of you are partially right or wrong.

For the first case, you should have the courage to accept that you are wrong. But if you don't know clearly whether you are right or wrong, then you have to listen to the other person with an open mind.

To do this, first you need to stop defending yourself upfront, come forward voluntarily, and listen to the other person's arguments. From their perspective, you might actually be wrong.

Then there comes the second part of the problem. That is when the second person is either entirely wrong or partially wrong. You need a different set of skills to resolve this conflict.

But before preaching to others, first you have to make sure that you are actually right. If it is some well-known or scientifically proven fact, then it would be an easy thing to do.

But arguments don't always happen over well-known facts. It might be some personal issue between two people. In this case, you can discuss with two or more people who are honest, trustworthy, and mature enough to give you directions.

If they also think that you are wrong, then you should really reconsider your thoughts. After confirming that your perspective is right, you need to do the hard part.

That is teaching the other person about their mistakes in the most kind and respectful way. In reality, this is more difficult than accepting your own mistakes. You need to be both emotionally mature and good at communicating your thoughts.

What do I mean by emotionally mature? A lot of the time, people don't want to accept their mistakes, and also some of them might really believe that their ideas are genuinely right. You need to be mature enough to convey their mistakes without pointing fingers at them.

The framework has really become helpful for me personally. I started using it whenever I got into an argument with my brother or friends. It reduced conflicts and helped me learn new things.

But there can be some exceptions. Sometimes both of you might be right based on your own values and perspectives. In those situations, both of you should respect each other's ideas and maybe could find a common ground to stand on.

Finally, arguments are not fundamentally bad, but the way you approach them could be. So just because it's hard, don't avoid them. To get something good out of it, you need to learn to listen to others, accept your mistakes, and teach the other person with kindness and respect.
