# **The Scale To Measure Closeness Of Two People**

### **The Road To Everlasting Relationships**

Recently something got into my mind. Is there a way to measure how close we are to someone? At first glance it seems to be a very simple question. But as you start to dig into it, it will uncover a lot of surprises.

You see people tell a lot of fairy tales about soulmates. They say soulmates will understand each other without even communicating, they will connect with each other the moment they meet, and all the other stuff. But in my opinion, these are all absurd.

People underestimate the power of communication by exaggerating this idea. We use the term soulmate to express how close two people are. So to understand this, first think about the idea of closeness.

In a way, closeness tells how resilient the relationship is. This is not only specific to romantic relationships. When we say a relationship, people assume it to be a romantic one.

But in reality, this applies to all kinds of relationships. It could be parent-child, siblings, cousins, friends, and also romantic relationships.

You see, this closeness is a peculiar thing about the relationship. To understand this better, first let me tell you a scenario.

There is a married couple who does know everything about each other. There are no secrets between them. Even when there is a mistake on one of them or when they feel guilty about something, they share it with each other.

Also, there is another couple who always have to play guessing games about each other. They don't communicate properly, and they have to hide a few things from each other to make it work. When one of them finds out one of these secrets, they will get into conflicts, which might also be resolved.

But on a random day, an unknown person comes into their life and accuses one of them of cheating. Which couple do you think will be more likely to be affected by this? Obviously not the first one. Why is that so?

If you concentrate clearly, then you could see that the first relationship is a stronger one. They are more resilient because of their trust in each other. The second couple might not have that much trust in each other because they usually don't share their secrets, mistakes, or guilt voluntarily.

You see, trust is the foundation of any kind of relationship. We have heard stories of friends trusting their lives with each other. So, based on trust, if people are betting their lives, you could understand the power of trust.

But how can we build this kind of trust? That's the tough part. The answer is simple to say but a very hard one to follow. It is honesty. It is the hidden gem of this story. But is it even practical to always be honest with everyone all the time? Sometimes, if a small lie can save a relationship, shouldn't we lie then?

The answer is that we can't do this with everyone. But if you want to be really close to a particular person, then you have to deliberately and consistently practice this with them. Even when it feels uncomfortable. To do this, first each one should try to acquire the following qualities:

* Listening to the other person with an open mind.  
* Being ready to forgive them if they actually did something wrong.  
* The person who did something wrong should be able to correct their mistakes and grow out of them.

Also, there is one more important aspect: honesty should be expressed from both sides. It should not be like one person always confessing everything to the other person, while the other person is always hiding theirs. It will only work if both of them are willing to follow this intentionally in the long term.

I want to state a quote from The School of Life here. Even though this quote is about teaching the other about their mistakes, it signifies the importance of mutual communication in a relationship.

*"Two people should see a relationship as a constant opportunity to improve and be improved. When lovers teach each other uncomfortable truths, they are not giving up on love. They are trying to do something very true to love: which is to make their partners more loveable."* *— The School of Life*

What would be the benefit of doing all this? After all, you will have to face a lot of unexpected consequences sometimes.

I know this is a hard road to follow. But the end is something more beautiful and long-lasting. It will unlock the true pleasure of having a true companion in your life.

So if you want to measure how close you are to someone, then count how many secrets you have hidden from them. Think about how honestly you could talk about your guilt, insecurities, and mistakes with them. By counting these things you can measure the closeness. That is the universal scale to measure closeness.
