How To Come Out Of Validation Seeking?  
Without Enough Attention, It Would Make Your Life Miserable

Seeking validation from others- have you ever thought about this? It destroys our lives in ways that we can’t even imagine. It can affect our lives in unexpected ways if we don’t pay enough attention to it.

Wanting validation is normal human nature, but it becomes an issue when it starts to excessively affect your relationships, decisions, and daily life in a significant and persistent way. The following checklist will help you understand your status.

* Being a people pleaser. Trying to be good to everyone all the time. It’s not the same as being kind to other people.  
* Constantly overthinking about each and every text reply or social media reaction from your friend or partner. When your friend doesn’t like your post, it becomes a war room inside your mind, analysing all the possible reasons.  
* Comparing yourself to others to know your self-worth. Your self-worth should not depend on how other people define their success.  
* Staying in an unhealthy relationship just because it approves of who you are. You define your self-worth based on whether someone loves you or gives you attention.  
* Trying to achieve some goal just for the recognition from it. You are not enjoying the achievement itself, instead a recognition that comes out of it. When you don’t get such recognition or reaction, you start to feel unhappy, anxious, or feeling empty in life.

Like this, this list is not exhaustive. But the most efficient way to figure this out would be to ask the following question.

**“Does your mood, happiness, decisions, actions, and daily life largely depend on other people's reaction or approval?”**

If the answer is yes, then you need to reconsider those behaviours. There are many clinical terms based on how this affects us in our day-to-day life, but all of them are commonly influenced by seeking validation or approval.

Why do we do this? Humans are social animals, so they live in groups. When a lot of people don’t agree with them or approve of their behaviour, it greatly influences their status in that group. So seeking validation makes them feel safe in a group.

Even today, it is true; the only difference is that we live in big societies. Also, we have constant influence and comparisons from social media. This has only amplified this effect. We attach our self-worth to these external validations.

Let me tell you my personal story about how I was controlled by this behaviour.

I have always been a person who seeks validation from others. When I was studying in high school, one time my cousin told me that I was not at the top of the class. I started worrying and crying about that. At that time, my self-worth was attached to being at the top of my class.

Unfortunately, I became the topper of my class that year, and for all subsequent years until college. Otherwise, I might have re-evaluated my self-worth at that time. 

In some way, the pressure from external validation kept me pushing to focus more on my studies. It was not a healthy way to achieve that goal. It made me constantly worry about things. Also, it was secretly waiting to destroy me.

To become a doctor was my only goal during my school time. At the end of my school days, I couldn’t become one. It destroyed me. I constantly worried about that as my failure. It largely affected my focus on my college education.

I was trying to cope with that stress by setting different goals and chasing different dreams. But I never realised this underlying issue. So it followed me wherever I went like a shadow.

But the real breakthrough came when I realised the following: I have always used external validation to define my self-worth.

If I had tried to fight this enemy without spotting him, I would not have known where to aim my weapon.

To understand how I tackled this, let’s take an example. So when I started writing on Medium, I asked myself, " Is this another way of me seeking external validation?

I do this for two reasons: one, to make myself a better person by improving my thought process and communication; second, trying to do something meaningful in this life that would help other people, even when I am gone.

I had to make sure that I would do this even when no one recognised me, or when people disagreed with me. Many such events happened during this writing process, but I still wanted to do this. That’s when I knew that I was doing this for myself.

You see, an old habit can’t be cut off just like that. Sometimes when I publish something, I check whether someone commented positively or gave claps for it. But I wanted to be careful about these observations. 

Consciously, I started making sure those reactions do not decide whether I write or not, the topics I write about, and the opinions I express. 

Similarly, while posting something on social media, initially I used to strongly value how people reacted to it. But now I tell myself that I am just using this tool to store my memories or best moments and nothing more than that. So whether someone likes it or not does not change that value.

It doesn’t mean you should not value other people's opinions, but rather it should not influence every decision you make in your life, and it should not define your self-worth.

Before ending this blog, I wanted to share a set of rules that would be helpful to tackle this.

* First, identify the situation. This needs conscious evaluation of your thoughts and actions.  
* When you feel sad, anxious, confused, or uneasy about something, is it because of other people's reaction or your own concern?  
* If it’s all about other people's reactions, approvals, or likings, then first acknowledge that feeling.  
* Reassure yourself that you do not always need external validation to be happy, make decisions, or live your life. Your life’s value doesn’t depend on them.  
* Take action based on how you actually feel about it, not because of external pressure.

What I am trying to say is that try to build your own identity, hobbies, and life values that would not largely depend on others’ reactions or approvals. Some practical steps could be as follows.

* Make small decisions on your own, like what to eat, what to wear, or which movie to watch, without relying on others.  
* When there is no reply from your friend, don’t overthink about it. Instead, focus on your own priorities.  
* Next time your boyfriend or girlfriend leaves you, or laid off from your job, don’t consider that the end of your life.

Like the above examples, re-evaluate your own actions and thoughts. When you free yourself from these external validations, you will start living a more authentic and happy life.
