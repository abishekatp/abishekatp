---
title: Hell Is Nothing But Heaven Is What He Hopes For
date: 2026-09-27
description: A Short Story Of Healing Hearts
---

The slight drizzle of rain had just stopped. Even the sun was getting ready to go back home.

She was walking beside him, holding his hand. Children were playing Tennikoit in the middle of the park. Turning her head all the way up, she looked at him and both smiled at each other.

They walked for a few more minutes. The half-dried bench was calling them out of its loneliness.

They had no other choice but to accept its calling. So that's what they did.

After a few silent moments looking at each other, a tiny drop of water fell on her cheek. She looked up trying to figure out where it was coming from.

He wiped that off her face. Suddenly she looked at him and asked, "Can I ask you something? But promise me you won't take it seriously."

"Yes, go ahead," he said, nodding his head.

"The first time we had a fight, I left you without any warning. What makes you believe that I won't do that again?" she asked, not looking away from him.

"What do you mean? Are you planning on doing that again?" he said, with a subtle smile on his face.

She was shaking her head for no. There was more than a single no in that shake.

"That's why I asked you not to take it seriously." she mumbled to him.

He looked away for a few minutes, looking at the people passing by them. Some of them were young couples, some of them were groups of uncles trying to keep their sugar under control, and some of them were groups of aunties trying to figure out everything that was going on in that neighbourhood.

Some children were playing and some were roaming around. A few parents were chasing them around to avoid any future misfortune.

He turned around, looked at her eyes for a few seconds, and said the following.

"I could never say that it was easy to get over you. If I am honest, for many days I was present in the moment without living one."

"Really? I knew it. I am really sorry for that. Then why?" she asked him.

"Don't be sorry, it was no one's fault. Life teaches us things in the most unexpected ways."

"But I could have handled it better, don't you think?" she said.

"Maybe, or maybe not, I don't know actually. You see, after all, we are all living this life for the first time. We can't guarantee that we will never make mistakes. So all that matters is how we handle those mistakes, isn't it?" He explained this, trying to console her.

A little girl interrupted while they were talking. She tugged at her fingers, asking her to play with her. Her parents were trying to take her away so as not to disturb them. She sat on her small bicycle and started pedaling it.

"Life would have been simple if we never grew up, wouldn't it be? We wouldn't have had to think about all these things," she asked him.

"Yes, of course, but that's what I am talking about. Life does not always go our way. So instead of trying to control it, let it guide us towards the light."

"Suppose if I had never come back, what would you have done?"

"Let me ask you something: would you ever want me to stay in that miserable self forever?"

"No, not at all. I would have actually wanted the opposite of that."

He nodded yes and said, "I would take the risk of entering that hell a thousand times for you. But I would want to cross that hell all those thousand times for myself."

"You talk all philosophical, but no good comes out of it," said she, while giggling at him.

"Fine, it's getting late, let's get back home now," he said to her.

Both started walking away from that bench. A slight, cool breeze hit the tree, and a few more drops of water shed onto that bench, getting ready to hear the next story.
