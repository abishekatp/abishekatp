Once I asked myself, if I had to teach my children only one skill, then what would it be? I think it should be rational thinking.

Rational thinking may seem like a simple term; you can ask people to think and make decisions.

But the process behind it has many things working against it, like incorrect information, illogical reasoning, emotional attachment to ideas, and more. These influences can cloud our decisions.

In this blog, we will explore why we need this skill and how we can practice it.

### It Started Way Before

It has been critical for our survival throughout history; even when we were just hunter-gatherers, we needed this skill to figure out where to stay, what to eat, when to move, etc. Is it relevant today? Let me tell you a story.

When I was in college, I wanted to start my own business. At that time, I followed some fake gurus with fake promises. Later, I asked myself, how could I even trust what they said? It made me feel dumb.

Business isn’t the only playground for them; we might face them in many paths of our lives, like spirituality, travelling, employment, science, and many other things.

We believe them so easily because they don’t usually tell an entire lie to us; instead, they hide some truths and sugarcoat a few assumptions to convince us.

While persuading us, they will use well-known facts and things that we can easily relate to our real life. They will build their own imaginary world on top of those partial truths.

Rational thinking is a tool that could help us in these kinds of situations. 

By using it, we could avoid those who try to influence or manipulate us for their own advantage.

The irony is, sometimes they don’t even gain any advantage by doing this; they just get lost in their own imaginary world, which they will try to sell to others for their own satisfaction alone. They are also victims of the same crime that they are committing against us.

### Impact of Internet & AI era

For a few reasons, this skill has become even more critical in this Internet and AI era.

First, on the Internet, subjective realities can be shared as objective truths by anyone effortlessly. It has tremendously increased the scope and weight of influence available to us.

An American or British guy who gives relationship advice might not always be correct for someone living in India or China.

An Indian finance influencer might not know that there is less than 1 percentage inflation in China, so their opinions might not be suitable for someone in China.

This is true in all aspects, not only for geographical and economic differences. 

Second, nowadays we have started relying heavily on AI chatbots to make our day-to-day decisions. It is heavily degrading our thinking capability, a separate topic for another day.

But also, there are a lot of fallacies in this process; the chatbot can hallucinate, might not have full context, and might give some generally accepted answers based on the data used during the training or available on the internet now.

That’s why I said this skill is even more critical in today’s world. Now, how can we practice it?

### The Framework for Rational Thinking

We could define a framework for this. But keep in mind, you don’t have to do all these things in just a few hours or minutes. Also, you don’t have to do them in the same sequence.

Some decisions can be made in seconds, and some may take days or months. You should patiently analyse and follow the process instead of being urged to make immediate decisions.

Instead, you will be jumping back and forth between these steps before making any conclusion. For example, in the practical thinking step, you might want to make some corrections to your information step.

Let’s take an example to better explain each of these steps. Suppose someone comes and tries to convince you that a particular race or caste is superior to another.

If you are not a rational thinker, there is a pretty good chance that you will be convinced by these people and their reasoning. Instead, before making any decision, you can use the following steps.

### 1\. Information gathering

The first step is to gather information about the thing that you are trying to decide. Don’t always trust all the information given by the person who is trying to convince you.

Instead, go to the library and read the history, use the internet, and ask opinions from the people whom you trust.

A white supremacist and a casteist might say that a particular group of people are uneducated and criminals. We would see that some of these people are actually not well educated and maybe convinced by their reasoning.

But here, it’s only a partial truth; that’s where the second part comes in.

### 2\. Critical Thinking \- Evaluating Information & Reasoning

Analyse the correctness and trustworthiness of the information available. Think about what parts of the information are just assumptions rather than facts. 

Discard all the false information. Be aware of the influence of your assumptions on your decision. Ask what will happen if those assumptions are wrong.

For example, consider this statement: they say this group of people is not well educated and does illegal things.

If you had gathered enough information from history, then you would come to know that the particular group was intentionally suppressed from getting an education for a long time. 

Also, there are different past and present social conditions for the current situation. So even though that education part might be true, there is another side to that story. 

First, those are not inherent qualities. Second, if given a chance, every human can learn and be better. The latter is actually the inherent quality of the majority of human beings, with very few exceptions due to various medical conditions.

### 3\. Logical Thinking

We have collected our own set of information about the situation and evaluated it.

Now, using your logical reasoning, you can spot flaws in their ideas and reasoning. Also, you can try to construct logical reasoning for your own conclusion.

For example, since they didn’t have proper education previously, they might have committed relatively more crimes than others; that doesn’t mean they are inherently bad.

Also, just because someone from a particular group did something wrong, you can’t generalise that to the entire group. 

So there are flaws in both information and reasoning. So now what are you going to do about it? If you want to just make the most logical conclusion, your search might end here. 

If you are someone who wants to take action and make changes, then you need the next step.

### 4\. Practical Thinking \- The Caveat

You see, the previous step might give you the most logically correct answer. There is no use of electricity without inventions like lights, motors, and all kinds of electrical and electronic devices.

When you are a purely logical thinker, you might have to reject some ideas that are accepted by the majority. If you try to apply those ideas directly, then there will be a lot of resistance.

If you don’t consider the practical consequences of your actions, then a lot of times you might end up hurting yourself or someone around you. It could even be someone you are trying to help.

A person who is only rational but not practical may seem arrogant and disliked by many. Some may face great losses by not being practical.

Instead, you can define which of your facts and assumptions are practical in today’s world and what is the most practical way to achieve your end goal.

A lot of revolutionaries and heroes would come under this category. They don’t act naively; instead, they plan the most systematic and feasible way to achieve their goal.

For example, Dr. B. R. Ambedkar didn’t fistfight with everyone who rejected his ideas; instead, he educated himself, rose to political power, and created systematic solutions to achieve his goals.

The world usually likes and appreciates a practical thinker more than a purely logical one. 

### 5\. Making a Decision

After considering information, reasoning, logic, and practicality, you could come to a conclusion. This conclusion will give you a final decision or a proper plan to move forward. 

In our example, we can reject those ideas about racism and casteism based on this process.

But it doesn’t end here. You are not actually a rational thinker without the next step.

### 6\. Revision Loop

One important quality of a rational thinker is being always ready to challenge our ideas, either by ourselves or by other people. 

Each time we are introduced to new information and ideas, it could change our reasoning and conclusions. 

So, we should be humble enough to accept our mistakes, and then be ready to change our decision.

### Conclusion

Finally, these days I don’t trust any influencers or ideas just by watching a single reel or video.

When someone is trying to convince me about something, I don’t want to agree or disagree instantly and blindly. Though it needs conscious effort from my side every time.

Instead, I try to observe them for some time, try to gather and analyse some facts, and follow the above process before making any concrete decisions or actions.
